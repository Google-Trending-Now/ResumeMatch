How Applicant Tracking Systems Actually Read Your Resume

Most mid-size and Fortune 500 employers in the United States use an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) such as Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, or Taleo to organize incoming applications. When you submit a resume, the software strips out visual styling and converts your document into structured plain text—sorting your contact details, job titles, employers, dates, and skills into searchable database fields.

Contrary to popular myth, very few hiring teams rely on a robot to automatically reject resumes based on a hidden percentage score. What actually happens is simpler: recruiters search their applicant pool by keyword. When 300 people apply for one opening, a recruiter types two or three must-have skills—such as "SQL," "HubSpot," or "budget forecasting"—into the ATS search bar to find qualified candidates first.

If you have the experience but used different phrasing than the hiring team, your resume can sit unseen on page four of the search results. Using a free ATS resume checker to compare your wording against the job description helps you mirror the exact language recruiters type when filtering applicants.

What trips up most ATS parsers

  • Multi-column tables and text boxes Older parsers read left-to-right across the full page, scrambling text inside sidebars or decorative tables.
  • Contact info hidden in document headers Some systems ignore Word header and footer zones. Place your email, phone number, and LinkedIn URL in the main body at the top.
  • Non-standard section titles Stick to plain labels like "Experience," "Skills," and "Education" instead of creative titles like "My Career Story."
  • Images, charts, and skill bars An ATS cannot read text embedded inside a graphic or interpret a five-dot rating bar next to "Excel."

5 Quick Tips to Improve Your Match Score Without Keyword Stuffing

Recruiters spot awkward keyword lists immediately. Use these practical edits to raise your ATS score while keeping your resume natural to read.

  1. 01

    Mirror the exact job title and tool names

    If the posting asks for "Customer Success Manager" and your last company called you a "Client Happiness Specialist," include the standard title in parentheses or your summary so both the ATS and the hiring manager recognize the fit right away.

  2. 02

    Pair acronyms with their spelled-out terms

    Write "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)" or "Amazon Web Services (AWS)" on first mention. That single habit ensures you match whether a recruiter filters for the acronym or the full phrase.

  3. 03

    Put missing hard skills inside achievement bullets

    Instead of dumping 20 terms at the bottom of the page, tie each keyword to a real outcome—for example, "Built weekly pipeline dashboards in Tableau, cutting leadership reporting time by 4 hours a week."

  4. 04

    Focus on repeated terms first

    When a job description mentions a tool or method two or three times, it is usually a core requirement for the role. Prioritize the red high-frequency missing keywords before worrying about one-off mentions.

  5. 05

    Only add skills you can discuss in an interview

    Treat a resume keyword matcher as a memory jogger, not a script to fabricate experience. Aim for a realistic 75% to 85% match built entirely on work you can back up with concrete examples.

What is a good ATS resume match score to aim for?

Aim for 75% or higher on the hard skills, tools, and job-specific phrases that genuinely reflect your background. You do not need 100%—most hiring managers expect candidates to meet around 70% to 85% of a role's listed qualifications.

Do Applicant Tracking Systems automatically reject resumes with low scores?

Most major US systems like Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, and iCIMS do not auto-reject resumes based on a keyword score alone. Instead, recruiters search the candidate pool using specific skill keywords. Including accurate terms from the job description makes sure your resume shows up when a recruiter filters applicants.

Should I include both acronyms and full terms on my resume?

Yes, when a term is central to the job. Writing "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)" or "Customer Relationship Management (CRM)" once ensures your resume matches whether a recruiter searches for the full phrase or the abbreviation.
